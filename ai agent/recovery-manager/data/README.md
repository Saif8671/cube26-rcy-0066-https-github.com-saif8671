# Reference data: Recovery Manager

> ⚠️ **DUMMY DATA.** Every row is synthetic, generated by a seeded script. The SKUs, ASINs, FNSKUs, orders, suppliers, operators, requirement flags and money amounts are all invented. Nothing here is a real Amazon rule or fee. Don't train on it, and don't treat it as ground truth.

**File:** `fee_report_sample.csv` (61 rows)

`upstream/` holds the other four pods' sample files: receiving, prep, pack and returns. Join on `unit_id` to decide whether the evidence **contradicts** a charge, **supports** it, or is **silent**. The sample includes all three cases, but it holds no answers: you have to work them out. Expect the real record shape to differ, because the pods agree it in week one.

Use it to design your schema, your structured output and your record page, and to see how your stage connects to the others through `unit_id`. Your **eval set is separate**: 50 units your agent has never seen, labelled independently by two humans.

The values `uncertain` and `pending_review` appear on purpose. Engineering rule 4 treats *uncertain* as a first-class verdict.

## Columns

| Column | Meaning |
|---|---|
| `line_id` | One line of a channel report. |
| `report_type` | `fee_report`, `inventory_adjustment`, `reimbursement_report` |
| `unit_id / org_id / sku / fnsku / fba_shipment_id / order_id` | Keys for matching the line to the other pods' evidence. |
| `charge_type` | `inbound_defect_fee`, `lost_inbound`, `damaged_in_warehouse`, `fulfilment_fee_weight_tier`, `refund_issued_item_not_returned` |
| `quantity` | Units the line covers. |
| `amount_usd` | **Invented amounts, not real Amazon fees or reimbursements.** Look the real schedules up. |
| `posted_date` | The date the line was posted. Inbound defect fees post about six weeks after prep. |

Found something inconsistent? That's a finding. Open an Issue labelled `finding`.
